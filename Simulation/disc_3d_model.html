A protoplanetary disc, from dust to planets

A 3D model of the flared gas disc around a one solar mass star. Dust settles towards the midplane at every radius according to the project's advection–diffusion solver. Where the midplane dust-to-gas ratio passes 1, planetesimals form and gather into a planet.

What the solver says at each radius

RadiusMidplane εDust layerSt₀Steady ε, exactStatus

ε is the midplane dust-to-gas ratio; it starts at 0.01 everywhere. The dust layer is the r.m.s. height of the dust in gas scale heights H. "Steady ε, exact" is the analytic balance between settling and turbulence that the solver should approach. With turbulence, ε passes 1 only when St₀/α is about 10⁴, which squeezes the dust into a layer 100 times thinner than the gas.

No planet is drawn at 100 AU. Dust can still reach ε > 1 there and make a planetesimal belt, but building a planet from planetesimals takes a time that scales roughly as 1/(ΣdustΩ), which in this disc grows as R3.25. That is about 17,000 times longer at 100 AU than at 5 AU, far beyond the disc's 1 Myr lifetime.

Taken from the project

The star is 2×10³⁰ kg. Temperature follows 264 K (R/1 AU)−1/2 and midplane gas density 5×10⁻⁷ kg m⁻³ (R/1 AU)−3, so the scale height H = cs/Ω flares outwards: H/R is 0.035 at 1 AU and 0.11 at 100 AU. Gas sits in a Gaussian of width H.

At 5, 10, 20, 30 and 100 AU the page solves the project's advection–diffusion equation with the same scheme as the corrected dust_functions.py and diffusion_functions.py: first-order upwind settling and conservative diffusion, with zero flux through the midplane and the top. To resolve dust layers as thin as 0.01 H it uses 200 cells that are finer near the midplane and takes the diffusion step implicitly. Those five runs produce the table and decide when ε passes 1. A run lasts 20 Myr, well past the 1 Myr a real disc survives; each radius is held fixed after 12 response times, 12 / (St₀ + α), once it has stopped changing.

Added for the picture

Each amber grain follows the same settling velocity and a random walk matched to α, at its own radius. Radii are drawn compressed (as R0.6) so 5 AU and 100 AU fit in one view, heights can be stretched, and the orbital motion is slowed and not tied to the clock.

The project's model ends when ε passes 1. What follows here is a sketch of the accepted sequence: the streaming instability makes planetesimals, they collide and merge into a protoplanet, and the planet clears a gap. Its timing on screen is not calculated.
